# Narrative Bible — The River Beneath the Mountain

Status: fully authored narrative baseline, including complete paired text and production registries; gameplay implementation remains future work. Development language is English; every player-facing string is paired in English and Simplified Chinese resources. This document defines canon for all scripts.

## 1. Dramatic premise

An apprentice is told to repair two failing spiritual anchors. Each successful repair steadies the mountain but increases pressure inside a guardian who has sustained its protective array for thirteen years. The apprenticeship becomes an investigation into a temporary emergency measure that became a permanent institution. Ascension means learning to share responsibility for a living landscape, not simply becoming powerful enough to command it.

The plot asks: when a safeguard protects people by hiding its cost, who gets to decide whether it should continue? There is no evil species, inherited destiny, resurrection, or last-minute villain beyond the evidence already available.

## 2. Physical and magical rules

The valley's Fivefold Array redirects excess spiritual flow through Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, and Earth regulators. Bamboo Hollow is its intake/buffering anchor; Ashen Peak is its release/heat-dissipation anchor. The sanctuary arena is the governor between them. A healthy open cycle returns cooled energy and water to the inhabited lower valley. An emergency closed cycle keeps catastrophic surges off the floodplain but draws more pressure into the governor.

Spiritual imprints are recorded patterns, not living souls. An echo cannot observe the present, answer arbitrary questions, or be resurrected by restoring its pattern. It can replay a record and reveal details as the player repairs a reader. Shen Heng, the bound guardian, is a living mind sustained within the governor; his damaged body is gone. He can briefly respond through the current array because he is not a recording.

In a functioning open cycle, passive Fivefold regulators share ordinary load. The closed emergency loop trapped overflow and required a living governor to compensate continuously. Returning Rain restores and tests that physical open cycle, with verified relief capacity and bounded allocations; named crews keep its regulators inspected. Signing a charter does not itself produce magical energy. Open River deliberately keeps Core regulation as a temporary safeguard while that network is completed.

The player's Golden Core can bridge the governor long enough to replace its unsafe control loop. It cannot solve unlimited shortages, bring back dead people, or make maintenance unnecessary. The final battle disables a defensive shell; it does not automatically kill Shen. All endings release him from the thirteen-year obligation. Whether he chooses to remain briefly as a fading imprint is his decision, never the player's property.

## 3. Timeline and causality

| Time | Event | Evidence and consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thirteen years earlier | A White Tide surge threatens the floodplain. Keeper Lan's younger sister Lan Mei dies guiding residents away from a broken spillway. | Her route log, a repaired lamp, and different accounts make her a person rather than a reward token. |
| Same emergency | Warden Shen Heng volunteers to act as temporary governor for thirty days. Yun, then a junior surveyor, signs the closed-cycle calculations. | The thirty-day limit is inscribed in both regulator archives. |
| Following years | Successive River Compact officers extend the emergency because replacing the system would expose risk and require shared maintenance. | Dated renewals, unfilled handover fields, and declining downstream records. No current council NPC or new city map is required. |
| Three years earlier | The adaptive governor begins reducing lower-valley winter allocations to keep the sanctuary stable. | Water marks, waterwheel records, and frost-damaged offerings. |
| One season earlier | Lan opens a small undocumented relief bypass after official requests fail. It helps residents but further strains the buffer. | His tool marks and a maintenance ledger. He admits the act before the archive reveals the full system. |
| Six days before opening | Yun leaves to warn and evacuate lower-valley communities, taking available senior cultivators. He leaves dated sealed instructions. | His absence has a practical cause; the apprentice is entrusted with local repairs, not selected by prophecy. |
| Present campaign | Reconnection stabilizes both anchors but pushes the governor near failure. The player discovers the old limit and chooses a replacement after surviving the storm. | Every chapter changes the player's understanding of the initial repair objective. |

The River Compact archives use a local register calendar: the White Tide occurred in Flood Year 400 and the campaign begins in Year 413. This is a fictional record convention, not a Gregorian date or a date tied to the real release. Relative dates in the reading resources refer to the same thirteen-year interval. The first-winter epilogue explicitly advances to the winter after the decision.

Lan's bypass contributes to the current failure but did not create thirteen years of exploitation. Yun knew the original emergency design and its renewals but underestimated its human cost; his letters acknowledge responsibility without demanding forgiveness. Shen accepted one month, not indefinite captivity. The River Compact is a flawed institution represented through records, not an enemy faction the player must exterminate.

## 4. Cast and voice

### The Apprentice

An adult former supply porter who joined the sanctuary two years ago, with no special lineage. Competent with paths, tools, and practical observation; inexperienced in cultivation. Give the player short, specific responses: ask for evidence, express concern, or commit to an action. The protagonist is not silent, but does not narrate every feeling. Profile name affects the UI label only; voiced/text dialogue uses Apprentice rather than inserting a potentially ungrammatical name.

Arc: useful obedience → uncertain investigator → informed responsibility. The player can be compassionate, skeptical, or practical without changing the factual revelations. Choices affect commitments and epilogues, not an invisible virtue score.

### Keeper Lan

Sanctuary caretaker and former spillway worker. Blunt, attentive to physical details, avoids institutional euphemisms. He keeps his sister's lamp functioning because it is useful, not because he believes it contains her. His concealed bypass is disclosed voluntarily when the first repair causes pressure symptoms. His conflict is fear that reopening the system will repeat the flood that killed Mei. He must offer reasons for caution without blocking truthful evidence.

### Elder Yun

Absent mentor, former surveyor, one of the emergency design's signatories. Delivery: dated letters, archived notes, and a late epilogue letter. No present-time remote omniscience. Starts with practical repair instructions, then an explicitly sealed acknowledgment accessed when the archive opens. The final letter was written before departure and is stored with the ending instructions; it does not react to a choice he could not know. Any future letter after his return belongs only to an epilogue with an explicit elapsed time.

### Warden Shen Heng / The Broken Guardian

The thirteen-year governor. Former field warden; remembers promises and names, not abstract statistics. Speech is initially interrupted by control commands, then becomes precise as readers are repaired. His boss shell follows safety logic even when he asks it to stop. He asks the player to finish the replacement, not blindly kill him. He can refuse to remain as a recorded consultant; that consent is fixed before the player's final choice.

### Lan Mei

Deceased spillway guide. Appears only through an authored route note and a short recorded fragment. Her everyday impatience, practical instructions, and unfinished plans prevent idealized sainthood. Restoring her lamp or record does not resurrect her. Her notebook reveals where people actually walked, informing the safe spillway route rather than an unrelated magical key.

### Reed Courier and Stonewright records

Optional side stories use correspondents through delivered notes and simple existing props. They require no extra animated humanoid or off-map city. Communities can agree to shared maintenance through pre-existing recorded responses contingent on choosing a documented proposal; a letter arriving in the present requires elapsed hub time and an explicit delivery transition.

## 6. Ending contract

Three clearly previewed choices appear after the final shell is defeated and Shen's release is secured. There is no hidden morality score, random ending, or paid/collection-completion ending.

- **END_OPEN — Open River:** reopen the balanced cycle with the apprentice's Core serving as a temporary regulator. The valley regains flow sooner; the apprentice commits to seasonal work while rebuilding shared maintenance. This is hopeful but keeps a concentrated burden. Always available.
- **END_SEAL — Quiet Summit:** safely retire the mountain intake, using the Core to drain the remaining reservoir before sealing. The old guardian is released; the sanctuary loses its spiritual abundance, and lower-valley water arrangements require relocation/alternative channels already prepared by Yun. This avoids another sudden flood and relinquishes the institution's power. Always available; not a punishment ending.
- **END_SHARED — Returning Rain:** reopen through a verified maintenance network. Requires `sq_spillway_verified`, `sq_allocation_agreed`, and `sq_handover_accepted`. Each unlock condition is displayed in the journal as soon as the proposal is known. Communities assume defined physical maintenance duties; the Core is no longer the sole ongoing governor. The system still needs work and does not restore lost lives. No claim that every character survives unharmed or that institutions become perfect.

All three require the same final combat and preserve post-ending realm replay as a clearly labeled reconstructed training expedition if the physical ending no longer permits literal visits. Optional quest outcomes select short epilogue paragraphs; they never contradict the chosen water-system outcome.

The final preview identifies consequences, commitments, and requirements. The player may return to the pre-trial hub to finish optional tasks before confirming. Confirmation commits the ending once. New Game is needed to choose a different ending; the game explains this before confirmation. No save deletion or hidden locking.

## 7. Production constraints

Reuse the existing hub, Bamboo rooms, Ashen rooms, and trial arena. Story visits may use safe-state versions with different props/lighting/enemy lists; do not turn evidence collection into repeated full expeditions. No new compulsory enemy archetype, free-flight mechanic, city, multiplayer feature, voice actor, or real-time fluid simulation is introduced.

The narrative scope expands the earlier short reading target. Main critical dialogue targets 35–50 minutes including interactions; optional text adds 25–40 minutes. Updated campaign expectation: 4–6 hours first clear, 6–8 hours with all side stories, to be measured. Scenes are skippable with equivalent journal/objective updates; skipping never auto-selects a consequential choice.

## 8. Script and text data contract

Every scene specifies ID, location, entry prerequisites, props/staging, exact player text keys, speaker/order, interactions and choice branches, objective updates, flags, completion conditions, rewards where relevant, skip/resume behavior, and subsequent scene. Every player-facing key has reviewed English and Simplified Chinese values.

Keep authorial production notes English. Runtime scripts reference keys and flags, not Chinese literals. Scenes use the main registry as authority. Side quests use a separate registry and cannot overwrite a main flag. Recorded text is labeled with its date/context; present dialogue cannot retroactively know later discoveries.
